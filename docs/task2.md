# Task 2: Multi-City AI-Based Traffic Congestion Analysis & Comparative Visualization
## Chennai, Vellore, and Coimbatore Traffic Intelligence Platform Extension

---

## 1. Task 2 Objective
The objective of Task 2 is to architecturally extend the existing Chennai-only Traffic Intelligence Platform into an enterprise-grade, scalable, **multi-city traffic intelligence and comparative analytics platform** covering:
1. **Chennai** (Metropolitan coastal capital; high density, multi-radial network)
2. **Vellore** (Transit corridor & educational/medical hub; regional highway transit nexus)
3. **Coimbatore** (Tier-2 industrial & IT growth center; dual-arterial radial network)

The platform enables traffic management authorities to:
- Dynamically switch between cities to inspect city-specific road networks, congestion severity, incident hotspots, and machine learning forecasts.
- Conduct cross-city comparative analysis using executive KPIs, 10 interactive statistical and machine learning visualizations, cross-city diurnal heatmaps, and evidence-based dynamic insights.
- Deploy city-specific machine learning models for short-term congestion forecasting across +15, +30, +45, and +60-minute horizons without future temporal leakage.

---

## 2. Why Multi-City Analysis Was Chosen
Urban traffic dynamics vary significantly across metropolitan hierarchies. While Chennai represents a high-density, radial-ring coastal metropolis with commuter peak congestion, Coimbatore functions as a high-velocity industrial manufacturing hub with dispersed logistics demand, and Vellore serves as a strategic regional bottleneck connecting interstate highways (NH48) with institutional healthcare radials.

Analyzing these cities side-by-side demonstrates:
- **Scalability of Data Architecture:** Seamless ingestion of multiple spatial graphs and disparate arterial topologies.
- **Model Portability vs. Localization:** Validating whether machine learning models perform better when trained on unified regional data or dedicated city-specific feature distributions.
- **Comparative Policy Benchmarking:** Enabling transport authorities to benchmark congestion indices, speed deficits, and incident frequencies against peer urban centers.

---

## 5. Data Classification & Provenance
To ensure complete scientific integrity and transparency, every data point and visualization element in the platform is strictly categorized:
- **`OBSERVED`:** Real-world sensor measurements and empirical historical traffic data (verified Task 1 Chennai baseline).
- **`SIMULATED BENCHMARK DATA`:** Deterministically generated synthetic traffic observations for Vellore and Coimbatore based on real GIS geometries and realistic traffic physics (Lighthill-Whitham-Richards flow theory). Clearly tagged to prevent misrepresentation.
- **`DERIVED`:** Deterministically computed mathematical aggregates (e.g., Congestion Index, Speed Deficit, V/C Ratio, Pearson Correlation, K-Means cluster assignments).
- **`PREDICTED`:** Short-term future traffic projections generated by trained machine learning regressors (+15m to +60m horizons).

## 7. Road Network Methodology
- **Real Coordinate Geometries:** All corridor centerlines represent genuine geographic road centerlines extracted from Tamil Nadu GIS highway inventories and OpenStreetMap.
- **Coordinate Precision:** Coordinates are stored in standard WGS84 (`EPSG:4326`) format as GeoJSON `LineString` and `Point` features.
- **Topological Integrity:** Each corridor is attributed with length, speed limit (km/h), vehicular capacity (veh/hr), zone designation, and cardinal flow direction.

---

## 8. Traffic Data Methodology
- **Deterministic Calibration:** Synthetic benchmark data generation utilizes fixed random seeds (`seed=101` for Vellore, `seed=202` for Coimbatore) ensuring 100% reproducibility.
- **Temporal Structure:** Full 24-hour diurnal profiles per corridor, partitioned into 15-minute sampling intervals across multi-day benchmark horizons.
- **Physics-Informed Relationships:** Speed-density relationships strictly enforce traffic flow conservation ($q = k \cdot v$), ensuring that as volume approaches capacity, speed degrades non-linearly towards congestion.

---

## 9. Machine Learning Methodology
- **Model Family:** Scikit-Learn `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` (histogram-based gradient boosted decision trees).
- **Inference Latency:** Sub-10 millisecond execution per corridor query, optimized for real-time spatial dashboards.
- **Defensive Fallback:** Automated fallback to persistence baselines if inference artifacts become unavailable.

## 11. Feature Engineering (27 Features)
Features are computed per corridor and timestamp strictly using past observations:
1. **Lagged Congestion:** $CI_{t-1}, CI_{t-2}, CI_{t-3}, CI_{t-4}$
2. **Lagged Speed:** $V_{t-1}, V_{t-2}, V_{t-4}$
3. **Lagged Volume:** $Q_{t-1}, Q_{t-2}, Q_{t-4}$
4. **Temporal Encodings:** Cyclical sine/cosine of hour ($H_{\sin}, H_{\cos}$), day of week ($DOW$), peak hour indicators ($IsPeakMorning, IsPeakEvening$)
5. **Rolling Historical Statistics:** 1-hour and 3-hour moving average, variance, and minimum speed (strictly excluding current timestamp $t$)
6. **Corridor Metadata:** Capacity, speed limit, road type one-hot encoding, active incident count

---

## 12. Prediction Target
- **Primary Target:** Congestion Index ($CI \in [0, 100]$) projected at horizon $t + \Delta t$.
- **Auxiliary Projections:** Expected operating speed (km/h) and vehicular volume (veh/hr).

---

## 13. Forecast Horizons
- **+15 Minutes:** Tactical junction dispatch and signal timing adjustment.
- **+30 Minutes:** Standard routing advisory and arterial load balancing.
- **+45 Minutes:** Strategic corridor diversion and incident queue management.
- **+60 Minutes:** Pre-emptive metropolitan demand management.

---

## 14. Train / Validation / Test Strategy & Leakage Protection
- **Strict Chronological Splitting:**
  - Training Set: First 70% of chronological timeline
  - Validation Set: Intermediate 15%
  - Test Set: Final 15% held-out chronological evaluation
- **Zero Future Leakage:** Random train/test shuffling is forbidden. Rolling aggregations use closed-left historical intervals.

## 16. City Comparison Methodology
Cross-city comparative metrics are calculated dynamically across all cities for any active hour:
- **Average CI Comparison:** Direct benchmark of network congestion severity.
- **Diurnal Profiles:** Comparison of morning (08:00–10:00) and evening (17:00–20:00) peaks.
- **Speed Deficit:** Deviation of operating speed below corridor design speed limits.
- **Capacity Utilization:** Total network volume relative to cumulative link capacity.
